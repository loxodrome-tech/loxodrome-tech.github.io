Marine navigation electronics

Instruments built to hold a bearing.

Loxodrome Tech designs open, field-ready NMEA 0183 hardware — readers, links and buffers — for the navigation systems that keep vessels on course, from the chart table to the engine room.

Explore the hardware github.com/loxodrome-tech

Hardware

Three problems, three instruments.

Every board reads or repeats the NMEA 0183 bus that most marine sensors still speak — the differences are where it sits in the chain.

Under the hood

From schematic to enclosure.

Every NMEA Link is designed from scratch — custom schematic, 4-layer routing, galvanic isolation on the RS-422 input — then built into a compact handheld enclosure with its own display.

Who's behind it

Built by someone who reads the bus for a living.

Loxodrome Tech is run by Matías Scuppa, an electronic engineer with over a decade in naval electronics and technical project management — the kind of hands-on experience that comes from keeping real navigation systems running on real vessels.

Every board is designed the way a technician wants to find it in a panel: documented, repairable, and honest about what it does.

  • Electronic Engineer — 10+ years across naval electronics and technical project management.
  • Systems — radar, GPS, AIS, ECDIS, gyrocompasses, VDR, VSAT, anemometers, compasses.
  • Hardware worked on — Sperry Marine, Furuno, SAAB, Raytheon Marine, Garmin, Wärtsilä, Terma, NACOS Marine.
  • Previously — led the Radar & Navigation Systems Section at the Naval Arsenal of Mar del Plata, Argentine Navy.
  • Based in — the south of France, on the Mediterranean coast.
